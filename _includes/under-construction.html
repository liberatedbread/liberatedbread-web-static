WHY IT IS DRAWN AND NOT A REAL GIF ---------------------------------- The obvious way to do this is the actual period artifact, and it was tried. It does not survive contact with the licensing: * The GeoCities-era construction.gif files — the ones everybody remembers — are orphan works. Everybody's homepage had one, almost nobody knows who drew it, and none of them is licensed for a commercial site to redistribute. That is the same rule the device guides follow for manufacturer photos. * Wikimedia Commons has freely-licensed ones, and the best of them is a trap: a scrolling bulldozer banner tagged CC BY-SA 4.0 as own work, whose own embedded GIF comments read "Guest: NON-COMMERCIAL USE ONLY" and name animationlibrary.com as the archive it came from. The page and the file disagreed; the file is the one that counts. This site carries affiliate links, so non-commercial terms are terms we would be breaking. Drawing it also buys things the GIF could not: it is ~2 KB of markup and no HTTP request on every page of the site, it scales to any size without going mushy, and — the one that matters — the motion is CSS, so `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` can freeze it. An animated GIF ignores that media query outright; the best you can do is serve a different file. THE ANIMATION ------------- Three frames, held and snapped between with `steps(1, end)` rather than eased — a 90s GIF was a handful of cels on a fixed delay, and a smoothly interpolated swing reads as a modern loading spinner instead of a throwback. Two joints, because one does not look like digging. Rotating the whole upper body at the hip (which was the first attempt) swings the figure upright and reads as a man walking with a briefcase. Someone digging stays hunched and moves the spade: the hip therefore travels a little (-5deg to +13deg) and the arm a lot (-12deg to +7deg), so the blade lifts clear of the spoil heap and comes back down into it. Angles are in src/input.css. Every pose is bounded by the sign it sits in. The triangle narrows fast going up, and lifting the arm swings the blade further RIGHT as it approaches horizontal, so a bigger raise puts the spade through the black border. The three poses were checked against that edge at render size, which is why the raise looks conservative on paper. NO IDs, ANYWHERE. This badge renders twice on an unverified device guide — once in the status block, once in the footer — and duplicate ids are invalid HTML. Nothing here is referenced by id, so it can repeat freely. ACCESSIBILITY. The {%- comment -%} The sign. Both triangles are drawn as a filled path PLUS a round-joined stroke of the same colour: that is what rounds the corners, and it is why each has a stroke-width at all. The yellow one is the black one scaled about its centroid, so the border reads as an even width all the way round. {%- endcomment -%} {{ uc_label }}
